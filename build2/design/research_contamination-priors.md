## SUMMARY
I established real-world calibration anchors for a UCDP-GED-driven landmine prior. The single most important finding is a ~1,400x gap between Ukraine's POTENTIALLY hazardous area (156,000 km2 in Apr 2024, reduced to ~139,000 km2 by Jan 2025, ~23-25% of the country) and its confirmed+suspected antipersonnel mine contamination reported under Mine Ban Treaty Art.5 (35.22 km2 at end-2023; 105.88 km2 in Landmine Monitor 2025) - the model must show both layers and never conflate them. Observed mine density converges on ~4-13 mines/hectare inside real patterned minefields (Falklands: ~30,000 mines across 23 km2 released; global 2024: 105,640 AP mines destroyed over 175.39 km2 of cleared mined land = ~6/ha), with extremes of 48-60/ha in the Cambodian K5 belt and a reported peak of 5 mines/m2 in parts of the Russian Surovikin line. Crucially, area-wide Ukrainian clearance yields only 0.72 explosive items/hectare (29,860 items over 41,243 ha, H1 2025), and only ~2.75% of surveyed land in Kharkiv/Mykolaiv proved confirmed-contaminated - so the prior should be a LOW base rate with a heavy tail, not a uniform smear. For predictors, the literature supports static defensive engineering (trench belts, border/ceasefire lines, prolonged occupation) over raw battle deaths; UCDP one-sided violence is a near-useless predictor and in Ukraine is only ~225 of ~31,547 GED events (0.7%), which I verified by parsing the user's actual ged251-csv.zip. Published spatial scales are multi-scale ring buffers: RELand uses 0.5/2/8/16/32 km at 500 m cells (ROC AUC 92.9%, PR AUC only 29.0%), Desk-AId uses 50/500/5,000 m buffers at 50 m granularity in Afghanistan with UCDP events as a feature but only AUC ~0.64 out-of-sample. The sector's standard guardrail is IMAS "all reasonable effort"/"residual risk" language, and RELand's authors deliberately withheld their public interface because a predictive minefield map is dangerous in the wrong hands.

### [verified] Extreme tail: Ukraine's Surovikin line (directly relevant to the robot's AO)
Russia reportedly emplaced 'up to five mines per square metre' in some sections = 5,000,000 mines/km2 locally - a hyperlocal peak, not an areal average. ### [verified] Doctrinal density definitions (US Army FM 20-32) for the robot code
LINEAR DENSITY = average number of mines in a 1 m wide path through the minefield's depth, anywhere along the front (mines per metre of front). AREA DENSITY = average number of mines per square metre anywhere in the minefield. Worked example: 100 mines over a 200 m front = 0.5 mines/m of front; the same field over 20,000 m2 = 0.005 mines/m2 (= 5,000 mines/km2 = 50/ha). Tactical effect design targets: DISRUPT and FIX ~50% probability of mine encounter; TURN ~80%; BLOCK >=80%. The manual states explicitly: 'These are guidelines, not fixed rules.'
_source: https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/policy/army/fm/20-32/chap2.html_

### [verified] AREA-WIDE density - what a robot sweeping suspected land will actually encounter
Ukraine MoD demining units, Jan-Jun 2025: cleared 41,243 hectares and found/neutralised 29,860 explosive devices => 0.72 devices per hectare = 72 devices per km2. That is ~8x LOWER than the in-minefield rate (~6/ha) because most released land is cancelled or low-density battle area. This is the correct base rate for the robot's expected encounter rate on generic suspected land, and the right scale for the likelihood of 'sweep found nothing'.
_source: reasoning from https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/ukraine/2025/07/ukraine-250701-ukraine-mod01.htm_

### [verified] RELand (Colombia) - the flagship academic paper on predicting landmine locations
Dulce Rubio et al., 'RELand: Risk Estimation of Landmines via Interpretable Invariant Risk Minimization', ACM Journal on Computing and Sustainable Societies (2024), arXiv:2311.03115. 500 m x 500 m grid cells, 70 tabular features in 3 groups: (1) Remnants of war - distance to cocaine crops (UNODC 2021), deforestation (Hansen 2013), illegal mining (2019), power/telecom/oil infrastructure (OSM 2022), historical landmine events (DAICMA 2022); (2) Geographic - altitude, rainfall, temperature (WorldClim 2023), soil texture, animal density (IUCN 2023), land use, topographic relief (IGAC 2022), distance to rivers; (3) Sociodemographic - population and night lights (WorldPop 2023, Li 2020), Meta wealth index 2022, distance to settlements/schools/roads/financial institutions, municipal GDP, conflict victim counts. Historical features computed at MULTIPLE BUFFER RADII: 0.5, 2.0, 8.0, 16.0, 32.0 km. Model: modified TabNet with a single global sparse mask (SparseMax) + Invariant Risk Minimization loss, 2 decision steps. Performance: ROC AUC 92.90%, Precision-Recall AUC 29.03% (within-department); out-of-department ROC 93.71% but PR AUC only 10.42%. Deployed with a humanitarian demining NGO in Colombia.
_source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.03115 ; https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3648437_

### [verified] Desk-AId (Afghanistan) - the paper that actually uses UCDP conflict events as a landmine predictor
'Desk-AId: Humanitarian Aid Desk Assessment with Geospatial AI for Predicting Landmine Areas', arXiv:2405.09444. Primary case study Afghanistan, follow-up in Cambodia. Ground truth = 12,098 confirmed-hazardous-area polygons from Afghan mine action authorities. Explicitly uses the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP) dataset - 'events of organized violence with at least one casualty' - to derive distance-to-conflict-event and estimated death counts, NOT ACLED. Base feature set (7): distance to buildings, roads, waterways, national borders; population density; elevation; hill slope percentage. Extended set (18) adds distance to financial services, education, health facilities, airports, controlled areas, conflict events. Minimum granularity 50 m, chosen as acceptable 'for areas with high erosion factor after many decades'. Negative sampling: HARD NEGATIVE MINING with buffer rings at 50 m, 500 m and 5,000 m around known hazard polygons plus country-wide random points (the hybrid gave the best trade-off). Results: macro F1 0.9 country-wide, but only AUC 0.640 (500 m buffer) and 0.636 (5,000 m buffer) on rural Study Area 1, and worse on urban Kabul. Graph Neural Networks with distance-weighted edges beat feedforward nets, especially at <=50 m sample spacing.
_source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.09444 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09444v1_

### [likely] Other prior art on landmine risk mapping
Earlier work used logistic regression and SVM for landmine risk maps in Bosnia and Herzegovina and Colombia; a logistic geographically weighted regression (GWR) spatial model raised ROC AUC from 0.84 (aspatial) to 0.89, showing spatial autocorrelation terms materially improve landmine prediction. A 2024 Journal of Location Based Services paper combines SVM + Random Forest + XGBoost with military expert knowledge and spatiotemporal data to predict landmine locations and classify regions by clearance priority and difficulty. NEC Laboratories Europe + the Cambodian Mine Action Centre (CMAC) report a proof of concept predicting landmine-contaminated areas in Cambodia with a 'match rate exceeding 90% against actual landmine locations', using CMAC records of detected landmine locations, resident-provided information, and open data on rivers, mountainous terrain and factory/critical building sites. Note the NEC release carries no caveat about not replacing survey or clearance.

### [likely] Which conflict features actually predict mines - the core modelling judgement
Minefields are an ENGINEERING product of force protection, not a by-product of lethality. Strong predictors: (a) static frontlines / trench systems / defensive belts - Ukraine's worst contamination concentrates in Kharkiv, Donetsk, Kherson and Mykolaiv, explicitly 'in areas affected by prolonged ground fighting, occupation, and heavy shelling', and Kharkiv has the highest casualty count because of 'dense, complex contamination from shifting front lines'; (b) length of occupation - 'the situation is most difficult in the Donetsk region, as well as in the de-occupied areas of the Kharkiv, Kherson, and Mykolaiv regions, where the occupiers were present for a longer period'; (c) border / ceasefire lines - Cambodia K5, Zimbabwe cordon sanitaire and Turkiye's Syrian border are all barrier minefields and all in the 'massive' category; (d) sieges and fixed defensive perimeters. Weak predictors: mobile or raiding warfare (forces that keep moving do not invest engineer hours in patterned minefields) and above all ONE-SIDED VIOLENCE against civilians - massacres are carried out by infantry with small arms at population centres, not by combat engineers at defensive lines, so UCDP type_of_violence=3 warrants a near-zero weight. Important counterexample: in insurgencies the mine-laying logic flips to protecting economic assets, which is exactly why RELand's Colombia model is built on distance-to-coca-crops, illegal mining and deforestation rather than on battle intensity.

### [verified] UXO/ERW vs landmines - different hazard, different generating process
Cluster munition dud rates: ICRC says submunition failure has varied from 10% to 40% in recent conflicts; mine clearance specialists frequently report 10-30%; Russian cluster munitions used in Ukraine are reported at 30-40%; US DPICM supplied to Ukraine is claimed at <2.35% but historic data for the same submunitions shows 6-14%, and real-world rates rise with wind, soft soil and dense vegetation. Consequence: UXO/ERW density is roughly (rounds fired or sorties flown) x a 0.1-0.4 dud fraction, so it tracks artillery and airstrike intensity - which battle deaths DO partially proxy - whereas mine density tracks defensive engineering effort, which battle deaths do NOT. Evidence of the split: in Afghanistan in 2024, of 624 mine/ERW casualties, ERW caused 90% of incidents, against only 22 from antipersonnel mines, 19 improvised mines, 16 antivehicle mines and 7 cluster munition remnants. IMAS also separates the operations: Battle Area Clearance (IMAS 09.11) is clearance of areas 'where the hazards are known not to include mines' - battlefields, defensive positions, and sites where air-delivered or artillery munitions were fired or dropped - as distinct from mine clearance. Build TWO hazard layers.
_source: https://www.csis.org/analysis/cluster-munitions-what-are-they-and-why-united-states-sending-them-ukraine ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/cluster-bombs-munitions-ukraine-united-states-russia/ ; https://the-monitor.org/country-profile/afghanistan/impact ; https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/09-11/_

### [likely] Spatial scale and buffer radii actually used in practice
There is no single published canonical buffer. Three defensible anchors: (1) Academic multi-scale - RELand computes historical-event features at 0.5, 2, 8, 16 and 32 km rings; Desk-AId uses 50 m, 500 m and 5,000 m rings around confirmed hazard polygons and found its conflict-distance features gave only AUC ~0.64 at both 500 m and 5,000 m, i.e. conflict proximity alone is a WEAK fine-scale predictor. (2) Data-imposed - UCDP where_prec=2 already carries 25 km of positional uncertainty, so a kernel narrower than ~25 km is meaningless for those events; filter to where_prec<=2 (ideally 1) for the fine Ukraine grid. (3) Operational - IMAS 08.20 Technical Survey sets no global number, stating that 'Definitions of fade out and buffer zones should reflect the type of contamination present and the typical distribution and density of associated devices, based on evidence collected during technical survey and clearance operations, as well as information about tactics and use by combatants', and that National Mine Action Authorities should delegate fade-out and buffer decisions to the most appropriate level. Practical recommendation: 500 m-2 km for a fine Ukraine prior from where_prec=1 events; 5-16 km as the 'defensive depth' band, matching the reported 3-10 mile densely-mined depth forward of Russian strongholds; 25 km as the floor for coarse-precision events.
_source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.03115 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.09444 ; https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/08-20/ ; https://ucdp.uu.se/downloads/ged/ged201.pdf_

### [verified] Temporal persistence and decay
Mines do not self-neutralise. Falkland Islands mines laid in 1982 were still functional and required clearance through 14 November 2020 - 38 years - across 122 minefields and ~30,000 mines. Laos still holds an estimated 80 million unexploded cluster submunitions ~50 years after the conflict. Cambodian K5 mines from 1985-89 remain the world's densest contamination; plastic-cased mines are waterproof and survive tropical conditions, while metal- and wood-cased WWII-era mines in wet ground do corrode toward inert. Practical model: essentially ZERO natural decay over a 0-50 year horizon for post-1970 plastic-cased AP mines; the real decrement is CLEARANCE, not decay. Clearance rates to net out: globally 175.39 km2 of mined land cleared in 2024 and 1,114.82 km2 of land released by States Parties (~80% via non-technical survey cancellation), with 105,640 antipersonnel mines destroyed. Ukraine specifically released 28.37 km2 in 2024, had 512,000 hectares cleared by late 2025, and cut its PHA from 156,000 to ~139,000 km2 in a year. Global casualties in 2024: 6,279 (1,945 killed, 4,325 injured), highest since 2020, 90% civilian, 46% of civilian casualties children.
_source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_mines_in_the_Falkland_Islands ; https://www.hi-canada.org/en/news/Five-things-you-should-know-about-contamination-in-Ukraine ; https://the-monitor.org/online-reader/landmine-monitor-2025 ; https://www.mineactionreview.org/documents-and-reports/clearing-the-mines-2024/_

### [verified] IMAS 'all reasonable effort' and residual risk - the standard disclaimer vocabulary
Exact definitions to reuse. RESIDUAL RISK (IMAS 07.14 / 07.11): 'the risk remaining following the application of all reasonable effort to identify, define, and remove all presence and suspicion of mines/ERW through non-technical survey, technical survey and/or clearance.' LAND RELEASE: 'the process of applying all reasonable effort to identify, define, and remove all presence and suspicion of mines/ERW through non-technical survey, technical survey and/or clearance', with criteria for 'all reasonable effort' set by the National Mine Action Authority. 'ALL REASONABLE EFFORT' is applied 'when the commitment of additional resources is considered to be unreasonable in relation to the results expected' - and the standards note that achieving a 'tolerable' residual risk 'may need to be demonstrated in a court of law'. IMAS 08.20 defines SHA from INDIRECT evidence of the presence of EO and CHA from DIRECT evidence, and stresses that 'Physical evidence of the presence of Explosive Ordnance (EO) is the primary source of hard data for analysis about the nature and distribution of hazard items'. Survey findings are NOT clearance: clearance is 'removal and/or destruction of all specified EO hazards to a specified depth'.
_source: https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/07-11/ ; https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/07-14-01/ ; https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/08-20/ ; https://explosive-ordnance-risk-reduction-pub.gichd.org/fileadmin/uploads/gichd-erisk/Resources/Land_release.pdf_

### [verified] The sector's explicit warnings about predictive maps and AI
RELand's authors deliberately restricted access: 'The predictions of RELand should always be analyzed with available information and expert knowledge', and their web interface is 'not open to the general public for ethical concerns' because it would be 'dangerous if this information is used by people with the intention to harm communities'. Separately, Gasser in JMU CISR's Journal of Conventional Weapons Destruction 28.2 (2024) attacks 'dangerously uncritical promotion on social media of unproven artificial intelligence (AI) technology that is potentially hazardous, insufficiently tested, and unlikely to provide practical solutions in the field', frames the core test as 'Would you walk on land declared safe by an unproven technology, developed by enthusiastic proponents who do not have long experience in the world of mine action?', notes generative AI hallucination rates of 3-16% make it unsuitable for safety-critical decisions, calls for 'Standardized AI training data, and defined success criteria agreed by researchers and mine action organizations', and warns the sector is repeating exaggerated technology claims made 25 years ago. Consequence: a predictive heatmap is a DESK ASSESSMENT and task-prioritisation aid feeding non-technical survey; it can never cancel land, certify clearance, or declare an area safe.
_source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.03115 ; https://www.jmu.edu/news/cisr/2024/07/28-2/02-282-gasser.shtml_

## RECOMMENDATIONS
- Build TWO separate, separately-labelled layers and never one: (1) PREDICTED mine density (Bayesian posterior from GED prior + robot sweeps) and (2) REGISTERED/CONFIRMED hazards (deterministic dots). Also split PREDICTED into a landmine layer and a UXO/ERW layer, because they have different generating processes (defensive engineering vs artillery dud rate) and IMAS treats them as different clearance operations.
- Calibrate the prior in expected mines per hectare using three anchors: area-wide suspected land 0.72 items/ha (Ukraine MoD, 29,860 items over 41,243 ha, H1 2025); inside a confirmed generic minefield ~6 mines/ha (105,640 AP mines over 175.39 km2 cleared globally in 2024; Falklands cross-check 4.3-13/ha); patterned barrier belt 48-60 mines/ha (Cambodia K5, 3,000 mines per linear km over a 500 m swath). Use a lognormal or negative-binomial density so the 5 mines/m2 Surovikin-line extreme is representable.
- Set the base rate of 'cell is truly contaminated' inside a suspected zone to ~2-5%, not ~50%. Justify with 739 km2 confirmed out of 24,000 km2 surveyed in Kharkiv/Mykolaiv (2.75%) and the fact that ~80% of 2024 global land release was non-technical-survey cancellation. A low base rate is what makes the robot's NEGATIVE sweeps informative, which is the whole point of the Bayesian update.
- Weight UCDP events by type_of_violence: full weight to state-based conflict (1), reduced weight to non-state (2), near-zero to one-sided violence (3). In Ukraine this discards only ~225 of ~31,547 events (0.7%) while removing a real confounder on the global map.
- Do not use battle deaths (best/high/low) as a direct proxy for mine density. Use deaths for the UXO/ERW layer (artillery/airstrike intensity x a 0.1-0.4 dud fraction) and use event PERSISTENCE for the mine layer: for each grid cell, count the number of DISTINCT MONTHS with at least one GED event within the buffer. A cell contested for 20+ months is a trench/defensive-belt candidate; a cell with one high-casualty event and nothing else is a raid or massacre, not a minefield.
- Use multi-scale ring buffers as the literature does, not one radius: 0.5, 2, 8, 16, 32 km (RELand) for the coarse/global layer and 50 m / 500 m / 5,000 m (Desk-AId) for the fine Ukraine robot grid. Use 500 m cells for the district heatmap (RELand) and 50 m for the robot's local planning grid (Desk-AId).
- Filter on where_prec before building the fine grid. where_prec=2 means 25 km of positional uncertainty and where_prec>=3 means 'somewhere in this ADM2'. Use where_prec=1 for the Ukraine local grid (optionally 2 with a 25 km kernel); coarse events are acceptable only on the global overview. Also filter on event_clarity and date_prec.
- Apply essentially ZERO natural decay over a 0-50 year horizon (Falklands mines were live after 38 years, Laos after 50). Model the temporal decrement as CLEARANCE instead: subtract reported released area per oblast per year (Ukraine released 28.37 km2 in 2024, 512,000 ha cleared by late 2025, PHA cut from 156,000 to 139,000 km2) from the posterior expected count.
- Put standard IMAS language prominently on the HTML, visible without scrolling and repeated in any export consumed by the robot: the output is a desk assessment and task-prioritisation product supporting non-technical survey; it does NOT constitute clearance, land release, or certification that any area is free of explosive ordnance; only 'all reasonable effort' via non-technical survey, technical survey and/or clearance as defined by the National Mine Action Authority can release land; residual risk remains even on released land. Cite IMAS 07.11, 07.14 and 08.20.
- Use the Landmine Monitor severity bins for the global choropleth legend so the map is comparable with published ones: light <5 km2, medium 5-19 km2, heavy 20-99 km2, massive >100 km2 (seven massively contaminated states: Afghanistan, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Cambodia, Ethiopia, Iraq, Turkiye, Ukraine).
- Show Ukraine's two contamination numbers side by side with an explicit toggle or annotation: potentially hazardous area ~139,000 km2 (support of the prior) vs confirmed + suspected antipersonnel mine contamination ~35-106 km2 (mass of the prior). Showing only the first is the commonest way these maps mislead.
- For the robot's likelihood model, use a sensor detection probability well below 1 so 'swept, found nothing' reduces but never zeroes the posterior. Justify the conservatism with the Mykolaiv/Kharkiv finding that over 70% of assessed minefields contain tripwire, magnetic influence or seismic mines, which a simple sweep may fail to trigger or detect.
- Parse the CSV with a real RFC4180 parser (pandas, Python csv, Papa Parse), never a line/comma split. GEDEvent_v25_1.csv is 250 MB with embedded newlines and commas inside quoted source_article and where_description fields; a naive split produces garbage (I reproduced this failure).
- Embed geography at build time as simplified TopoJSON/GeoJSON inlined into the HTML (Ukraine oblast and raion boundaries plus world countries) and render with inline SVG or canvas, not a tiling map library. No runtime fetch of any kind; scripts only from cdnjs.cloudflare.com, cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm or code.jquery.com. Precompute the grid aggregates at build time so the 250 MB CSV never ships.
- Report top-k precision or PR AUC in the UI rather than ROC AUC. RELand reports ROC AUC 92.9% but PR AUC 29.0% in-domain and 10.4% out-of-domain; on a 2% base rate ROC AUC flatters the model badly and would overstate confidence to a robot operator.

## PITFALLS
- Conflating potentially hazardous area (139,000 km2) with confirmed hazardous area (~35-106 km2) in Ukraine. These differ by a factor of ~1,400. A heatmap that spreads the 139,000 km2 figure as if it were mined land will be off by three orders of magnitude and actively dangerous.
- Using battle deaths as the mine-density driver. Deaths measure engagement lethality. Minefields are laid by combat engineers where forces DEFEND, frequently with few or no deaths recorded at that exact spot - a well-laid minefield deters rather than kills, so the best-mined cells can be among the quietest in GED.
- Giving one-sided violence (type_of_violence=3) real weight. Massacres of civilians are committed at population centres with small arms; they predict UXO and IEDs at best, never patterned minefields. In Ukraine the category is only 0.7% of events, so excluding it costs nothing while the confounding it introduces elsewhere is real.
- Assuming Poisson-distributed mine counts. Real densities span 0.72 items/ha (area-wide) to ~6/ha (generic minefield) to ~60/ha (K5 belt) to a reported 5 mines/m2 (Surovikin line) - four orders of magnitude. A Poisson prior with one rate will systematically under-predict the barrier minefields that matter most and over-predict empty ground.
- Using a single buffer radius. Both published systems use multi-scale rings (RELand 0.5/2/8/16/32 km; Desk-AId 50/500/5,000 m) precisely because no single scale works, and Desk-AId's conflict-distance feature scored only AUC ~0.64 out-of-sample. Do not oversell conflict proximity as a fine-scale predictor.
- Ignoring where_prec. Many GED events are geocoded to an admin centroid. Kernel-smoothing those at 500 m resolution piles fictitious hazard onto town halls and district centres - exactly the populated places where a false positive does most damage.
- Splitting the GED CSV on commas or newlines. Quoted source_article and where_description fields contain both, and a naive parse silently corrupts year, type_of_violence and coordinates while appearing to work.
- Treating a high ROC AUC as proof of usefulness. RELand reports ROC AUC 92.9% but PR AUC of only 29.0% in-domain and 10.4% out-of-domain because contamination is extremely rare. On a ~2% base rate, ROC AUC is close to meaningless as a decision metric.
- Letting the predicted heatmap and the registered-mine dots be visually confusable. They must differ in mark type, colour ramp and legend, and the predicted layer must be labelled as an estimate everywhere it appears, including inside any file handed to the robot's trajectory planner.
- Modelling mines as decaying over time. They do not on any horizon this model covers; only clearance removes them. Conversely, forgetting to subtract clearance (Ukraine released 28.37 km2 in 2024 and cut PHA by 17,000 km2 in a single year) makes the posterior stale in the opposite direction.
- Merging landmines and UXO/ERW into one number. They need different clearance methods (IMAS separates Battle Area Clearance from mine clearance because 'clearance methodologies may differ greatly'), respond to different predictors, and in Afghanistan ERW caused 90% of 2024 casualty incidents versus 22 from antipersonnel mines. A planner that cannot tell them apart will plan the wrong sweep.
- Publishing a fine-grained public predictive minefield map without considering misuse. RELand's authors withheld their public interface because such information is 'dangerous if used by people with the intention to harm communities'. Consider coarsening the public view and reserving fine resolution for the operational export.
- Omitting or burying the IMAS disclaimer. The failure mode the mine action community fears most is a predictive map being read as clearance certification. Gasser's test - 'Would you walk on land declared safe by an unproven technology?' - must visibly be answered 'no; this is a survey prioritisation aid'.
- Quoting a single global 'mines per km2' constant. The FM 20-32 worked example (0.005 mines/m2) is an illustrative AT minefield, not a norm, and the manual itself says 'These are guidelines, not fixed rules'. Anyone citing it as the standard density will be wrong by an order of magnitude in both directions.
- Mixing denominators when deriving density. The 105,640 antipersonnel mines destroyed figure is for States Parties (Landmine Monitor) while the 175.39 km2 cleared figure is global operator output (Mine Action Review). The ~6 mines/ha result is a sound order-of-magnitude anchor but must not be presented as a precise measured rate.
