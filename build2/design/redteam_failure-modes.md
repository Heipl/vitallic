## SUMMARY
I loaded GEDEvent_v25_1.csv (385,918 global events; 31,547 Ukraine events, 245,068 deaths, 2014-2024) and measured the specific failure modes. The findings are worse than the task brief assumed. Only 23.0% of Ukraine events are exactly geolocated (where_prec=1); 53.3% are where_prec=2, which UCDP defines as "up to 25 km away" — a 1,963 km2 uncertainty disc. Worst: 1,194 events carrying 89,634 deaths (36.6% of ALL recorded Ukraine fatalities) sit at exactly (49.0, 32.0), the Ukraine country centroid in Cherkasy oblast, hundreds of km from any front. Any death-weighted KDE prior will put its single largest hotspot on uncontaminated central Ukrainian farmland. The top-10 coordinates hold 69.4% of recorded deaths; there are only 2,440 distinct coordinates for 31,547 events. A resolution-saturation test settles the precision question empirically: going from 0.05deg to 0.01deg multiplies cell count 25x but occupied cells only 1.22x (2,234 -> 2,346 from 0.025 to 0.01 is 1.05x). Median nearest-neighbour distance between distinct coded locations is 3.58 km. There is no information below ~0.05deg; 0.01deg output is fabricated detail. On the Bayesian update: a 0.01deg cell is 812,121 m2; an optimistic 10,000 m2 robot-day covers 1.23% of it and yields a Bayes factor of only 1.75 between "100 mines" and "50 mines" — and that is the Poisson-uniform figure, which overstates the evidence roughly 3x because minefields are laid in belts (clustered: P(zero detections) 0.975 vs Poisson 0.328). Sweeping all of Ukraine at that rate takes 165,356 robot-years. The benchmark for this exact task, RELand (Dulce Rubio et al., ACM JCSS 2024), uses 70 features, 500 m cells, and labels from real demining operations, and still only achieves PR-AUC 29.0% +/- 22.1 at a 1.6% base rate; its authors deliberately withheld public release. The single biggest design flaw is that the product outputs mine COUNTS per district, which nothing in the pipeline can calibrate and which dilutes lethal point risk by ~5,000x; the cheapest high-value fix is to add one feature (signed distance to the time-evolving line of contact) and reframe the output from counts to IMAS-vocabulary ranked survey priority.

### [verified] SEVERITY 1 (fatal): the product promises a quantity nothing in the pipeline can produce — mine counts
Deliverable A is specified as 'estimate 100 landmines in this district'. There is no path from UCDP GED to a calibrated count. A count requires a link function from event/death intensity to mines-per-km2 that has been fitted against ground truth (surveyed area with known mine yields). No such label set exists in this project. Any number displayed is an arbitrary scaling constant chosen by the developer, dressed as an estimate. Worse, the count framing dilutes exactly the risk that kills: 100 mines spread over a 0.25deg cell (507.6 km2, measured) reads as 0.197 mines/km2, which a reader will discount as negligible — while the physical reality is a single belt of maybe 10 ha at ~1,000 mines/km2. That is a 5,000x understatement of risk at the one location in the cell that matters. The count is not merely uncalibrated, it is actively anti-safety because it invites uniform interpolation.

### [verified] What where_prec=2 actually means — the 53% of Ukraine events you were going to trust
UCDP codes where_prec=2 when the source says the event was 'near', 'in the area of', or up to 25 km from a named location; the coordinates given are that named location, not the event. So the majority category in Ukraine carries a 25 km positional uncertainty radius = a 1,963 km2 disc = 2,418 cells at 0.01deg resolution. A where_prec=4 event in Donetsk oblast (26,517 km2) is consistent with any of 32,652 cells at 0.01deg. ### [verified] SEVERITY 1 (fatal): a defensible maximum resolution is 0.05 degrees — 0.01deg is fabricated detail, proven empirically
Resolution-saturation test on Ukraine events (occupied cells vs cell size): 0.5deg=225, 0.25deg=509, 0.2deg=637, 0.1deg=1250, 0.05deg=1922, 0.025deg=2234, 0.01deg=2346, 0.005deg=2394, 0.001deg=2434. Cell count grows as res^-2 but occupied cells saturate hard: 0.05->0.01 multiplies available cells 25x and occupied cells only 1.22x; 0.025->0.01 gives 1.05x. The curve flattens because the data is only 2,440 distinct coordinate pairs behind 31,547 events (12.9 events per coordinate). Median nearest-neighbour distance between distinct coded locations = 3.58 km (p25 1.97 km, p75 6.39 km). Conclusion: the intrinsic spatial resolution of this data is ~3-6 km. 0.05deg (5.56 x 3.65 km at 49N, 20.3 km2) is the finest defensible prior grid; 0.1deg is the honest display default. At 0.01deg the prior touches 2,346 of 743,507 possible cells (0.316%) and 41% of those hold exactly one event.

### [likely] The two-resolution rule the implementation must follow
The robot legitimately needs a fine local grid (10 cm - 1 m) for its own occupancy/evidence map. That is fine ONLY if the fine structure comes from the robot's sensors and terrain features, never from upsampling the GED prior. Concretely: inside one 0.05deg prior cell the GED-derived prior must be a FLAT CONSTANT. Any sub-0.05deg variation in the displayed map must be attributable to a non-GED source (robot sweep data, terrain, road network, front-line distance) and should be visually distinguishable from prior-driven variation. Bilinear/bicubic interpolation of the prior down to 0.01deg or finer is the specific thing to forbid — it creates smooth, confident-looking gradients that are pure interpolation artifact.
_source: reasoning from the measured saturation curve and the where_prec uncertainty radii_

### [likely] SEVERITY 1 (fatal): systematic displacement — mines mark where fighting was PREVENTED, not where it happened
The core causal premise is inverted for the highest-density contamination. Protective/defensive minefields (the Russian belts in Zaporizhzhia/Kherson are the canonical case) exist precisely to deny approach routes, and where they succeeded there was little or no recorded combat — no combat means no GED event. Nuisance/denial mining of border zones, rear approaches, treelines, canal and river crossings, and abandoned positions produces zero GED events by construction. Meanwhile GED events cluster on assault axes and urban battles (Bakhmut n=1,332 at one coordinate; Avdiivka n=642; Mariupol 27,412 deaths) where contamination is dominated by UXO and rubble rather than patterned minefields. So the GED signal is displaced from the densest minefields by roughly the depth of a defensive belt — typically 5-30 km on the defender's side of the line of contact. At the project's proposed 0.01deg resolution (1.11 x 0.73 km cells) a 10 km displacement is ~10-14 cells of pure error. The prior is not noisy around the truth; in the belts it is systematically offset, and the offset has a sign the model does not know.
_source: reasoning grounded in measured GED coordinate concentration (analyze2.py) plus IMAS 08.10 evidence taxonomy; the belt geometry is doctrinal, not measured here_

### [likely] SEVERITY 1 (fatal): the kernel-bandwidth bind — no smoothing width gives a useful product
I computed the union area of buffers around all 2,440 distinct Ukraine GED coordinates against Ukraine's government-stated potentially contaminated area of 139,000 km2 (23% of the country, revised down from 174,000 km2 in Sept 2024). r=1 km: 7,181 km2 = 1.2% of Ukraine, an area equal to only 5.2% of the national SHA. r=2 km: 26,090 km2 (18.8%). r=5 km: 101,006 km2 (16.7% of Ukraine, 72.7%). r=10 km: 207,398 km2 = 34.4% of Ukraine, 149% of the entire national SHA. r=25 km (the honest where_prec=2 radius): 456,250 km2 = 75.6% of Ukraine. So: a tight kernel produces an area budget far too small to contain the real contamination (catastrophic false negatives), and a kernel wide enough to be honest about positional uncertainty lights up three quarters of the country and discriminates nothing. There is no bandwidth that is both safe and informative. NOTE: this is an area-budget argument, not a measured overlap with real SHA polygons, which I do not have.
_source: Union-of-buffers computed on a 0.01deg grid over Ukraine in scratchpad\analyze3.py; 139,000 km2 figure from Government of Ukraine Sept 2024 estimate via web search (the-monitor.org, justsecurity.org)_

### [verified] SEVERITY 2 (severe): the Bayesian update is numerically inert — exact figures
A 0.01deg cell = 0.812 km2 = 812,121 m2. An optimistic UGV rate of 10,000 m2/day covers 1.23% of it; a realistic 2,000 m2/day covers 0.246%. Under Poisson thinning with detection probability 0.9, one clean (zero-detection) robot-day gives a Bayes factor of just 1.746 for 'N=50' over 'N=100' at 10,000 m2/day, and 1.117 at 2,000 m2/day. On the Jeffreys scale, BF < 3 is 'not worth more than a bare mention'. At coarser cells it vanishes: 0.05deg cell -> BF 1.022; 0.25deg cell -> BF 1.001. In Gamma-Poisson terms with a deliberately weak prior worth 0.5 km2 of pseudo-sweep, one clean 10,000 m2 day multiplies the posterior mean by 0.9823 (-1.77%); you need 56 consecutive clean robot-days to halve a cell's estimate, or 278 days at the realistic rate. Fully sweeping one 0.01deg cell takes 81 robot-days. Fully sweeping Ukraine at 10,000 m2/day takes 60,355,000 robot-days = 165,356 robot-years. Yes, the product will feel broken — because it correctly represents that one robot cannot learn about a landscape.
_source: Computed in scratchpad\analyze2.py 'BAYESIAN UPDATE ARITHMETIC' section_

### [verified] The Poisson model OVERSTATES the evidence from a clean sweep, because minefields are clustered
The thinning likelihood L(N) = (1 - f*p_d)^N assumes mines are uniformly distributed within the cell. Minefields are the opposite: they are laid in patterned belts and rows, so contamination within a cell is a few compact patches. Quantitatively, for a 0.01deg cell with 100 mines and a 10,000 m2 sweep: under Poisson-uniform, P(zero detections) = 0.328; if those 100 mines sit in a single 1-hectare patch, P(the sweep misses the patch entirely) ~ 0.975. So a clean sweep is roughly 3x less informative than the Poisson model claims, and the naive update will shrink risk faster than the evidence warrants — which is the dangerous direction of error. Use a clustered (Neyman-Scott / log-Gaussian Cox / Poisson-Gamma mixture with high overdispersion) likelihood, or at minimum inflate the variance so that negative sweeps move the posterior less.
_source: Computed in scratchpad\analyze3.py 'how clustered are mines' section; clustering geometry is doctrinal_

### [verified] Detection probability p_d cannot be treated as a constant near 1, and depth matters
IMAS 09.10 defines cleared land as removal/destruction of all hazards to a SPECIFIED DEPTH, with a default recommended minimum of 130 mm for metal-detector operations. A robot's p_d is mine-type and depth dependent and is far below 0.9 for minimum-metal ordnance: PFM-1 scatterable 'butterfly' mines are plastic and tiny, PMN-2 and many AT mines are low-metal, and deep-buried AT mines, mines under rubble, and mines below sensor depth have p_d effectively 0 for a single-sensor platform. A 'clean sweep' from a sensor that does not reach 130 mm is not evidence of absence for the hazard classes that matter. The model must carry a per-mine-type p_d vector with an explicit stated depth envelope, and the UI must state that a swept cell is swept FOR THE DETECTABLE SUBSET ONLY. Setting p_d = 1 anywhere in the code is a safety defect.
_source: IMAS 09.10 (mineactionstandards.org/standards/09-10/) fetched: clearance to specified depth, default minimum 130mm, two-stage QA+QC_

### [likely] SEVERITY 2: the honest fix for inertness — separate the fast-moving honest layer from the slow-moving risk layer
Do NOT manufacture responsiveness. Give the user a SWEPT-FRACTION / COVERAGE layer as a first-class, separately-rendered display. Coverage is deterministic, moves visibly with every robot pass, and is completely honest. Let the risk posterior move as slowly as the evidence actually warrants, and label each cell with its swept fraction and an explicit 'evidence so far: X m2 of Y m2 (Z%)' readout plus the posterior credible interval, which will stay wide. Pair this with a headline number the robot CAN move: area swept, anomalies logged, items confirmed. The user then experiences progress without being lied to about knowledge. Also show the posterior as an interval, never a point estimate — an interval that visibly fails to narrow is the correct and self-documenting representation of 'one robot is not enough'.
_source: reasoning from the measured Bayes factors_

### [likely] SEVERITY 2: spatial spreading of evidence — when it is legitimate and when it is fraud
Spreading a detection to neighbouring cells is legitimate IF AND ONLY IF the kernel bandwidth encodes a real belief about the spatial correlation length of mine-laying. That correlation is real — minefields are contiguous belts, so a confirmed mine at x genuinely raises P(mine) at x+50 m and along the belt axis. But the defensible correlation length is the scale of a minefield pattern: tens to a few hundred metres across the belt, potentially kilometres ALONG the belt axis (i.e. the kernel should be anisotropic and oriented, not isotropic). The test is simple and should be enforced in review: the bandwidth must be justified by mine-laying doctrine (row spacing, belt width, belt orientation), written down in the code as a named constant with that justification, and NOT tuned to make the heatmap visibly change. If anyone spreads a single detection over a multi-kilometre isotropic radius so the UI 'feels alive', that is fabricating evidence, and at the resolutions in play it will repaint hundreds of cells the robot never approached. Note the asymmetry: spreading POSITIVE evidence (a detection raises neighbours' risk) is conservative and defensible; spreading NEGATIVE evidence (a clean sweep lowers neighbours' risk) is the dangerous direction and should have a much shorter bandwidth, or be disallowed entirely.
_source: reasoning; grounded in the clustering geometry computed in analyze3.py_

### [verified] SEVERITY 2: the selection-bias loop — the UPDATE is valid, the VALIDATION is not
Precise statement, because the usual framing is muddled. If the robot is tasked using only the prior and previously observed data, the sampling policy is sequentially ignorable (MAR) for the likelihood: conditioning on where you swept, the update of THOSE cells is statistically valid. The bias enters in two other places. (1) Unswept cells never update and sit at prior forever, so the posterior map is prior-dominated everywhere except a vanishing measure-zero set, and the contrast between 'visited' and 'unvisited' will be misread as a finding. (2) Any claim of model VALIDATION from robot data is circular: 'we predicted high risk, went there, found mines' is unfalsifiable because low-risk cells were never sampled, so you can never observe the false-negative rate that actually matters. RELand's own field pilot illustrates the trap — it 'uncovered three mines in a high-risk area while finding none in a low-risk zone', which is n=2 areas and is an anecdote, not an evaluation.
_source: RELand paper (arxiv.org/html/2311.03115, ACM JCSS 2024) field-pilot description; MAR/sequential-ignorability argument is standard reasoning_

### [likely] Correct treatment of the sampling policy — concrete requirements
(a) Log the tasking PROPENSITY for every sweep: the probability that cell was selected at the time it was selected, stored with the sweep record. Without it no unbiased evaluation is ever possible retrospectively. (b) Mandate a randomised exploration arm: allocate a fixed fraction (10-20%) of sweeps to cells drawn by a randomisation scheme that gives non-zero probability to LOW-risk cells, stratified across the risk deciles. This is the only source of data that can falsify the model. (c) Evaluate only with Horvitz-Thompson / inverse-propensity weighting over the logged propensities, and report calibration (predicted vs observed hit rate per risk decile) rather than 'we found mines where we looked'. (d) Log ALL sweeps including nulls and including aborted/partial passes — silent dropping of null sweeps is the single easiest way to corrupt this dataset, and it happens by accident when only 'interesting' runs get saved. (e) Never let the displayed posterior be used as the propensity in the next evaluation without recording that dependency.
_source: reasoning; standard causal-inference treatment of adaptive/bandit data collection_

### [verified] IMAS vocabulary the tool must use — exact definitions
IMAS 07.11: Land release = 'process of applying all reasonable effort to identify, define, and remove all presence or suspicion of EO through non-technical survey, technical survey and/or clearance' (3.12). Non-Technical Survey = 'collection and analysis of data, without intrusive interventions, about the presence, type, distribution and surrounding environment of EO contamination' (3.15). Technical Survey = same but 'using appropriate intrusive interventions' (3.20). CANCELLED LAND = 'suspected or confirmed hazardous area concluded not to contain evidence of EO contamination, based on reliable information that meets established cancellation criteria' (3.2). REDUCED LAND = the same conclusion reached following intrusive work (3.16). CLEARED LAND = 'area where all specified EO hazards have been removed and/or destroyed to a specified clearance depth' (3.3). SHA = 'area where there is reasonable suspicion of EO contamination on the basis of INDIRECT evidence' (3.19). CHA = 'area where the presence of EO contamination has been CONFIRMED on the basis of DIRECT evidence' (3.4). IMAS 07.11 clause 4.2: 'An area shall not be defined or recorded as SHA or CHA if there is no indirect or direct evidence.'
_source: Fetched mineactionstandards.org/standards/07-11/_

### [verified] IMAS 08.10 places this project's output squarely in 'indirect evidence' — which licenses SHA, and nothing more
IMAS 08.10 classifies DIRECT evidence as visual observation of ordnance, detonations, mine signs, equipment, accurately-located incidents, visible IED components — this supports a CHA. INDIRECT evidence is verbal reports, unreliable records, analysis of known contamination patterns, FORMER COMBAT ZONES, and incidents with uncertain locations — this justifies an SHA at lower confidence. This is the key reframe for the whole project: 'there was fighting here' is explicitly recognised by IMAS as legitimate indirect evidence, which means a conflict-event model is a legitimate input to SHA definition and survey prioritisation. It is NOT a basis for a mine count, and it is categorically not a basis for cancellation. IMAS 08.10 is explicit: 'absence of evidence' alone cannot justify land cancellation; cancellation requires demonstrating that all reasonable effort was applied such that 'had contamination been present, the totality of efforts applied could reasonably have been expected to identify evidence'. A robot sweeping 1.23% of a cell fails that test by roughly two orders of magnitude.
_source: Fetched mineactionstandards.org/standards/08-10/_

### [verified] Accreditation: this system has no standing to produce survey output
IMAS 08.10 requires that the National Mine Action Authority (NMAA) accredit organizations to undertake non-technical survey, and that survey organizations 'shall gain accreditation needed to conduct non-technical survey'. Information sources (communities, former combatants, historical records) do not need accreditation — but the entity conducting survey does. A hobby/research robot running DimensionalOS is, at best, an information source feeding an accredited operator's NTS. The tool must therefore never render anything in the visual language of an IMSMA survey product, and must state its non-accredited status on the face of the UI, not in a footer.
_source: Fetched mineactionstandards.org/standards/08-10/ (accreditation requirements section)_

### [likely] SEVERITY 1 (fatal): what the tool must refuse to claim
Hard prohibitions, enforced in code and copy: (1) Never output 'safe', 'clear', 'cleared', 'cancelled', 'reduced', 'mine-free', 'no mines', or a green colour that reads as safe. There is no low-risk colour — the low end of the scale must be 'not yet surveyed / no data', not 'low risk'. (2) Never imply a route is passable. (3) Never present a swept corridor as cleared land; it is a sensor track. (4) Never use the words cancelled/reduced/cleared at all except in a glossary explaining that the tool cannot produce them. (5) Never claim the robot's detections constitute technical survey. The realistic harm from someone trusting a low-risk prediction: a farmer returns to a field, a child takes a shortcut, a returnee family clears rubble, a vehicle uses a track. Anti-personnel mine casualties are typically traumatic amputation; anti-tank mines kill vehicle occupants outright. The asymmetry is total — a false positive costs survey time, a false negative costs a leg or a life — and in Ukraine the exposed population is on the order of millions of returnees across a stated 139,000 km2. This is also why the count display is worse than a tier display: a number invites arithmetic ('0.2 mines per km2, I'll risk it'), a tier does not.
_source: reasoning grounded in IMAS 07.11/08.10 land-release semantics and the 139,000 km2 / 23% Ukraine contamination figure_

### [verified] Precedent: the state of the art for this exact task, and how much better-resourced it is
RELand (Dulce Rubio et al., ACM Journal on Computing and Sustainable Societies, 2024; arXiv 2311.03115) is the closest published system. It uses 500 m x 500 m cells over 15 municipalities in Antioquia, Colombia; 26,761 cells; ~70 features spanning topography, socio-demographics, infrastructure, and remnants-of-war. Critically its labels come from REAL demining operations (AICMA records: confirmed hazard areas, and negative labels only from areas thoroughly inspected and declared free of mines). Results: ROC AUC 92.90% +/- 4.43 but Precision-Recall AUC only 29.03% +/- 22.11, at a 1.6% positive base rate in-domain (0.42% out-of-department). The single feature 'distance to nearest historical LANDMINE event' alone reaches ~86% ROC AUC — note that is distance to prior MINE events, not to battle events; the authors explicitly treat over-reliance on it as a spurious correlation to be corrected via Invariant Risk Minimization. Their stated scope: 'intended to be used by demining organizations as input in their humanitarian post-war non-technical surveys and not as an automatic real-time decision system that declares areas as safe or unsafe.' And: 'We decided not to release our web interface to the public except for related non-profits. It would be dangerous if this information is used by people with the intention to harm communities.' Read against this project: 1 feature instead of 70, 0 labels instead of operational ground truth, 0.01deg instead of 500 m, and a public interactive HTML instead of a withheld one.
_source: Fetched arxiv.org/html/2311.03115_

### [verified] The PR-AUC lesson: report the right metric or the product will look far better than it is
RELand's ROC AUC of 92.9% next to a PR-AUC of 29.0% is the single most instructive number in the literature for this project. Under a ~1% base rate, ROC AUC is dominated by the enormous true-negative mass and looks excellent while the model is still wrong most times it cries wolf. If this project ever reports a performance number it must be PR-AUC or a calibration curve or a 'mines captured in top X% of area surveyed' curve — never ROC AUC, never accuracy. Given one feature and no labels, the realistic expectation is well below RELand's 29%.
_source: RELand metrics fetched from arxiv.org/html/2311.03115; base-rate reasoning standard_

### [likely] SEVERITY 3: reporting/media bias compounds the geocoding problem in a correlated way
GED is a reported-event dataset built from news wires, so density tracks journalist and stringer access, not combat. In Ukraine this means: occupied territory is systematically under-reported relative to Ukrainian-controlled territory; rural and treeline engagements are under-reported relative to named-settlement battles (which is exactly why 53% of events land on settlement names at where_prec=2); and casualty-heavy claims get aggregated to the country centroid. Crucially the bias is not random noise that averages out — it is correlated with precisely the covariate you care about (rear-area, low-visibility, defensive terrain is both under-reported AND more heavily mined), so it pushes the prior in a consistent wrong direction rather than just adding variance. Additionally, deaths are a proxy for combat lethality, not mining effort: an artillery duel producing 200 deaths may leave no mines, while a quiet engineer unit laying a 20 km belt produces zero GED events and zero deaths.
_source: reasoning grounded in the measured where_prec=2 settlement concentration and the 2,440-distinct-coordinates finding_

### [likely] The statistically correct handling of low-precision events: hierarchical, not point-smeared
Do not drop where_prec>=3 events (that discards real information about which oblasts were fought over) and do not smear them as points (that invents locations). Fit a hierarchical model: let where_prec 1-2 events drive the within-admin spatial field, and let where_prec 3-6 events contribute ONLY to a partially-pooled admin-level intercept (raion for prec=3, oblast for prec=4, multi-oblast region for prec=5, and DROP prec=6 entirely since a country intercept is uninformative and is the source of the (49.0,32.0) phantom). This is both statistically correct and it automatically deletes the single worst artifact in the data. It also makes the ecological fallacy explicit in the model structure rather than hiding it in a kernel.
_source: reasoning from the measured where_prec distribution and centroid artifacts_

### [verified] SEVERITY 4: dual-use and publication
A public, interactive, sleek map of inferred mine locations is dual-use. It assists scavengers and metal thieves (a leading cause of civilian mine casualties in some contexts), it has military value in inferring defensive works, and if it ever contained real confirmed coordinates it would be a targeting aid. RELand's authors explicitly withheld their web interface from public release on exactly these grounds. If this is published as an Artifact, it should be scoped as a methods demonstration on clearly-marked synthetic or coarse data, not as an operational map of Ukraine — and the decision to publish should be conscious rather than incidental.
_source: RELand release statement fetched from arxiv.org/html/2311.03115_

### [likely] WHAT A DOMAIN EXPERT WOULD SAY IS THE SINGLE BIGGEST FLAW
The design predicts battle intensity and relabels it mine risk, while omitting every feature that actually carries the mine signal — and then reports it as a count at a resolution the data cannot support. In mine action the unit of decision is a POLYGON with a provenance and a land-release state (SHA -> CHA -> reduced/cancelled/cleared), not a raster cell with a number. A product that emits numbers-per-cell cannot be consumed by the workflow it claims to serve, cannot be reconciled with IMSMA, and encourages precisely the uniform-density misreading that gets people hurt. Secondarily, the entire design has no ground truth anywhere in it: there is no point at which the model can be wrong in a way anyone would notice.

### [likely] CHEAPEST CHANGE THAT MOST IMPROVES REAL-WORLD VALIDITY
Add ONE feature: signed distance to the time-evolving line of contact, and reframe the output from counts to ranked survey-priority tiers. Mine-laying in Ukraine is overwhelmingly organised RELATIVE TO THE FRONT — belts run along and behind the line on the defender's side, typically offset 0-30 km — which is exactly the structure a KDE of battle deaths cannot represent, and it is the structure that explains the displacement problem in finding 7. Daily front-line polygons are publicly available (ISW, DeepStateMAP and similar), they are a single extra layer, and a signed, side-aware distance-to-front feature (plus its time derivative, since static fronts get mined and fast-moving ones do not) will outperform a death-weighted KDE by a wide margin for a fraction of a day's work. Second-cheapest and even higher value if obtainable: ingest real SHA/CHA polygons that ALREADY EXIST (Ukraine's SESU/MoD mine-danger mapping, HALO/FSD/DRC survey data flowing into IMSMA) — that both beats the model outright in the areas it covers and finally supplies labels against which the model can be evaluated. Third: replace the count with a 5-tier priority ranking whose only claim is ordinal ('survey this before that'), which is the claim the data can actually support and the claim IMAS 08.10 already licenses via 'former combat zones' as indirect evidence.
_source: reasoning; grounded in the measured displacement/saturation findings, IMAS 08.10 indirect-evidence taxonomy, and RELand's feature-importance result that proximity features dominate_

## RECOMMENDATIONS
- Kill the mine-count output. Replace 'estimate 100 landmines in this district' with a 5-tier ordinal SURVEY PRIORITY ranking. The ordinal claim ('survey A before B') is the only claim the data supports and is exactly what IMAS 08.10 licenses by naming 'former combat zones' as indirect evidence justifying an SHA. A count is uncalibratable and dilutes lethal point risk by up to ~5,000x.
- Set the prior grid at 0.05 degrees maximum (5.56 x 3.65 km, 20.3 km2 at 49N); prefer 0.1deg for display. This is not a style choice — occupied cells saturate at 2,234 (0.025deg) -> 2,346 (0.01deg), a 1.05x gain for 6.2x more cells, and median nearest-neighbour spacing of coded locations is 3.58 km. Forbid interpolating the prior below 0.05deg; inside a prior cell the GED contribution must be a flat constant.
- Drop all where_prec=6 events before doing anything else. 1,194 events carrying 36.58% of all recorded Ukraine deaths sit at exactly (49.0, 32.0) in Cherkasy oblast. Leaving them in puts your largest hotspot on uncontaminated central farmland. Verify by asserting in code that no single coordinate holds more than a few percent of total weight.
- Handle where_prec>=3 hierarchically, not spatially: prec=3 contributes to a raion-level partially-pooled intercept, prec=4 to an oblast intercept, prec=5 to a multi-oblast intercept, prec=6 is dropped. Only prec 1-2 events drive the within-admin spatial field. This is worth doing even though it is more work than a KDE — it is the difference between a model and a picture.
- Add signed distance-to-line-of-contact as a feature, with its time derivative. This is the single highest-value-per-hour change in the whole project. Daily front polygons are one extra public layer and they encode the belt geometry that GED events structurally cannot.
- Build a separate, prominently-displayed SWEPT COVERAGE layer driven by the robot. It is deterministic, honest, and moves visibly with every pass, which gives the user real responsiveness while the risk posterior correctly barely moves. Show posterior CREDIBLE INTERVALS, never point estimates, and print 'evidence: X m2 of Y m2 (Z%)' per cell.
- Use a clustered likelihood (Neyman-Scott / log-Gaussian Cox / heavily overdispersed Poisson-Gamma), not Poisson thinning. Poisson-uniform overstates the evidence from a clean sweep by ~3x (P(zero detections) 0.328 vs 0.975 for the clustered case), and it errs in the unsafe direction by shrinking risk too fast.
- Carry a per-mine-type detection probability vector with an explicit depth envelope, referencing the IMAS 09.10 130 mm default. Assert p_d < 1 everywhere. Label swept cells as 'swept for the detectable subset only'.
- Log the tasking propensity with every single sweep record at the time of tasking, and log ALL sweeps including nulls, aborts and partials. Without propensities no unbiased evaluation is ever possible retrospectively, and silent dropping of null sweeps is the easiest way to corrupt this dataset by accident.
- Mandate a randomised exploration arm of 10-20% of sweeps, stratified to give non-zero selection probability to LOW-risk cells. This is the only data that can falsify the model. Evaluate only with inverse-propensity weighting and calibration curves; report PR-AUC or mines-captured-vs-area-surveyed, never ROC AUC or accuracy.
- Put synthetic demo dots in a fictional coordinate frame or an unmistakably non-real location — never at plausible Ukrainian coordinates. Use a different GLYPH, not just a different colour, so the distinction survives screenshots, greyscale and colourblind viewers.
- Give every mine record a three-tier provenance enum (sensor_anomaly / operator_confirmed / EOD_disposed) and only draw the latter two as 'registered mine' dots. Make deliverable B's ingest THROW on any record that is synthetic or sensor_anomaly-only, rather than silently filtering it.
- Remove 'safe', 'clear', 'cleared', 'cancelled', 'reduced' and 'mine-free' from the entire vocabulary, and make the bottom of the risk scale read 'NOT SURVEYED / NO DATA' rather than 'low risk'. Do not use green anywhere on the scale.
- Put the data vintage ('conflict events through 2024', ~21 months stale as of Sept 2026) and the non-accredited status on the face of the UI, not in a footer. IMAS 08.10 requires NMAA accreditation to conduct NTS; this system is at best an information source feeding an accredited operator.
- Seek real SHA/CHA polygons (SESU/MoD mine-danger mapping, HALO/FSD/DRC via IMSMA). If any are obtainable they beat the model outright in the areas they cover and finally supply labels. A model with no possible ground truth is a model that can never be found wrong.

## PITFALLS
- Building a death-weighted KDE without filtering where_prec=6 first. This is the default, obvious implementation and it puts the single largest hotspot at (49.0, 32.0) in Cherkasy oblast — 36.58% of all recorded Ukraine death-weight on uncontaminated central farmland. The bug is invisible on a coarse global overview and glaring on a Ukraine zoom.
- Rendering at 0.01 degrees because the robot works at fine resolution. The robot's local grid and the GED prior's resolution are different things. Downsampling/interpolating the prior to match the robot manufactures smooth confident gradients that are pure interpolation artifact, and the saturation curve proves there is no information below ~0.05deg.
- Treating where_prec=2 coordinates as points. It is 53.32% of Ukraine events and UCDP defines it as up to 25 km away — a 1,963 km2 disc, i.e. 2,418 cells at 0.01deg. A 1-2 km kernel on these asserts positional knowledge off by three orders of magnitude in area.
- Tuning the evidence-spreading bandwidth until the heatmap visibly moves. This is the specific way this project will become dishonest, and it will feel like a UI fix rather than a scientific one. Bandwidth must come from mine-laying doctrine (belt width, row spacing, belt orientation) as a named, justified constant. Note the asymmetry: spreading positive evidence is conservative, spreading negative evidence is dangerous and needs a much shorter kernel or none.
- Using Poisson thinning for the update. It assumes mines are uniform within a cell, which is exactly backwards for belt-laid minefields, and it overstates the informativeness of a clean sweep by ~3x in the unsafe direction.
- Setting detection probability to 1, or to a single constant across mine types. Minimum-metal ordnance (PFM-1, PMN-2, low-metal AT), deep-buried AT mines and mines under rubble have p_d near 0 for a single-sensor UGV. A clean sweep from a sensor that does not reach the IMAS 130 mm default depth is not evidence of absence for the hazards that matter.
- Discarding null sweeps, or saving only 'interesting' runs. This happens by accident and silently destroys the only unbiased signal the robot produces.
- Claiming validation from robot data: 'we predicted high risk, went there, found mines'. It is unfalsifiable because low-risk cells were never sampled. RELand's own pilot (three mines in a high-risk area, none in a low-risk zone) is n=2 areas and is an anecdote — do not reproduce that as a success metric.
- Reporting ROC AUC. At a ~1% base rate it is dominated by true negatives and will read ~93% while the model is wrong most times it flags a cell. RELand's honest companion number was PR-AUC 29.0% +/- 22.1 with 70 features and real operational labels.
- Placing clearly-labelled synthetic dots at real Ukrainian place coordinates. The label does not survive a screenshot. Synthetic dots at Robotyne become 'confirmed mines at Robotyne' one repost later.
- Letting synthetic or sensor-anomaly records reach deliverable B through a silent filter rather than a hard validation error. A filter that quietly drops bad records also quietly drops them when the provenance field is misspelled.
- Using a green-to-red scale. Green reads as 'safe' regardless of the legend text, and there is no safe tier here — the low end is 'not surveyed', which is an absence of knowledge, not a presence of safety.
- Shipping the map with no visible data vintage. GED v25.1 ends in 2024 and today is Sept 2026; an undated map is read as current, and the newest contamination is in exactly the areas with zero data.
- Publishing an interactive public map of inferred Ukrainian mine locations without a deliberate decision. It is dual-use (scavengers, military inference, targeting if real coordinates are ever present). The closest published system deliberately withheld its web interface for this reason.
